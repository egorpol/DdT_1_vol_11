# DdT 1, Vol. 11 — 0.12.0 (pre-release)

This is the fourth public release of the MEI edition of *Dietrich Buxtehudes Instrumentalwerke* (Denkmäler deutscher Tonkunst, first series, vol. 11, ed. Carl Stiehl, 1903). It has the same 17 works as 0.11.0, each a complete MEI 5.1 file with every measure linked to the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek facsimile. New in this release are repairs of transcription errors in 48 bars where the continuo and the keyboard's left hand disagreed, an annotation on each of the 13 entries for Op. II and the appendix works in DdT's *Vorbericht*, further readings from the Düben manuscripts of the appendix works with the first five excerpts from them, and a rule for the accidentals that DdT prints in round brackets.

**Status: corrected, not finalized.** This is a review snapshot of 2026-11-10. The OMR text of every work has been corrected against the DdT 1903 page images, and the facsimile links have been reviewed. The correction is not complete: a comparison of two staves made after 0.11.0 found the 48 bars repaired here, and the staves that this comparison cannot reach have had no check of the same kind (see Known limitations). Publication sign-off, final filenames, the release-mode gate and whether 1.0 covers 16 or 17 works are still open. The [1.0 checklist](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/finalization-plan.md) lists what remains. The files can be used and cited now, but details may change before 1.0. Each change will be listed in the [changelog](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/CHANGELOG.md).

## Changes since 0.11.0

The [changelog](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/CHANGELOG.md) lists every change, work by work. This is a minor release rather than a patch because an event ID changes: in App. II m. 55, `r14shlwa`, a measure rest in 0.11.0, is now the continuo's C3 whole note, which the OMR had dropped. No note of 0.11.0 is gone. Measure IDs, measure numbers, measure zones and facsimile links are the same as in 0.11.0 in all 17 works.

### Continuo and keyboard left hand

In DdT the keyboard's left hand mostly doubles the continuo. A scan listed the onsets where the two staves of the encoding disagree: 60 onsets in 50 bars of 13 works, among 13,937 compared onsets. An AI model (OpenAI Codex) read both staves of each of the 50 bars from the DdT page image without seeing the encoding, and the editor checked all of these bars against the page images on 2026-11-09. 48 bars are repaired as DdT prints them, the continuo in 10 and the left hand in 38. In the other two, Op. I/1 m. 147 and Op. I/5 m. 147, DdT's own staves differ, and its reading stands. The repairs carry no annotations, because DdT is unambiguous at each note; each work's revision history and the changelog list them note by note.

This comparison sees only the notes that the continuo and the left hand share, and it cannot find a slip that both staves share. The other staves were not checked this way; see Known limitations.

### DdT's *Vorbericht*

DdT's *Vorbericht* lists doubtful passages on pp. VI–VII. Each of its 13 entries for Op. II and the appendix works now has an annotation that quotes the entry and links the page. The [README](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/README.md#editorial-approach) and the [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#sources-and-their-roles) now say how the list is used: it belongs to the DdT source, it points to readings but does not attest them, and no encoded reading rests on it alone.

- **The Op. II print:** source-reading annotations with excerpts at Op. II/3 mm. 47, 51, 86 and 89, Op. II/6 m. 49 and Op. II/7 mm. 1–2 and 35, and one without an excerpt at Op. II/4 m. 209. DdT's text is retained at each. These readings and the one at Op. II/5 m. 40 were made by an AI model (Anthropic Claude), with a blind second read that agreed on every note, and the editor chose each outcome on 2026-11-07. They are selective comparisons, not a collation.
- **Op. II/5, m. 40, violin:** the first two beats now fill the bar. F-sharp5 is a dotted eighth, as in the Op. II print, where DdT prints a dotted sixteenth, and C-sharp5 is an eighth, the edition's own conjecture. The pitches are unchanged. The annotation also gives the different repair that the *Vorbericht* proposes, and an excerpt shows the print.
- **The Düben manuscripts:** source-reading annotations with excerpts at App. I m. 83 and App. III mm. 92 and 130. DdT's text is retained at each, and the reading of the violone's last four notes and of the first two of its four marks at App. III m. 130 remains tentative.

### The Düben manuscripts

- **App. I:** the Uppsala manuscript Imhs 13:25 is recorded as a selectively examined source, with its three facsimile links. The dances were located and m. 83 was read in two parts; the source has not been collated.
- **App. II, m. 156, violin II:** the manuscript's D5 eighth followed by an eighth rest replaces the interim conjecture of 0.11.0, and the annotation is now a source-supported emendation.
- **App. II, m. 56, and App. III, m. 37:** source-reading annotations record figures in the manuscripts that differ from DdT's, whose figures are retained. At App. II m. 180 the manuscript evidence is added to the existing annotation; the F-sharp4 remains a conjectural emendation.
- AI models (Anthropic Claude, OpenAI Codex) read the manuscripts at full resolution, and the editor chose each outcome on 2026-11-06 or 2026-11-07, reading some places by eye. These are selective comparisons, not a collation.
- **Excerpts:** five one-bar excerpts, from App. I m. 83, App. II m. 156 and App. III mm. 37, 92 and 130, are published under Uppsala University Library's permission of 2026-11-06. Each is credited “Uppsala University Library” with its shelf mark (Imhs 13:25, 13:27, 13:24). The edition's licences do not cover them; see the [rights note](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/supplemental_material/README.md).

### Signs that DdT prints in round brackets

An accidental that DdT prints in round brackets on a note staff is part of DdT's text, and it sounds. It is encoded as `<accid enclose="paren">`, with `place="above"` where DdT prints it above the note; `func="edit"` stays reserved for the edition's own signs. A note that DdT prints in round brackets also sounds, and carries `head.mod="paren"`. The [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#recording-decisions-in-mei) states the rule.

- **App. III, m. 104:** the gamba's last eighth now sounds C♮5, not C♯5. DdT's bracketed natural above it was text in a `<dir>`, which does not sound.
- **Op. I/7, m. 62:** the sharp on the gamba's D4 is drawn once, in round brackets above the note, not a second time as a plain sharp beside it. The note still sounds D♯4.
- **Op. I/1, m. 59,** keyboard E♭4, and **Op. II/7, mm. 1–2,** gamba A4: the round brackets that DdT prints are now encoded. The notes sound as before.
- **Sources:** four `source-reading` annotations record that a historical source has no sign where DdT brackets one, and the edition keeps DdT's: Op. I/7 m. 62, Op. II/7 m. 167, and App. III mm. 29 and 104. An AI model (OpenAI Codex) read the sources from the scans, and a second model (Anthropic Claude) checked the crops.

### Other repairs

- **App. II, m. 55:** the continuo holds a C3 whole note, as DdT prints it (p. 167), not a rest.
- **App. III, m. 106:** the keyboard's left hand starts on A3, as DdT prints it (p. 180), not B3.

- **Op. II/4, m. 209:** the last three figures read 5, 6, 6, as DdT prints them (p. 124), not 6, 5, 6.

### Wording

Annotations in 16 works, the README and the viewer now call the keyboard realization DdT's: the viewer's Parts menu lists “Realization (DdT)”, and 51 annotation sentences read “The keyboard realization is DdT's, not Buxtehude's”. The volume's title page, *Vorbericht*, *Inhalt* and the heads of pp. 3, 79, 160, 164 and 176 name no realizer. No notes or IDs change.

## Using the edition

### Choosing staves for analysis

The files keep DdT's printed score, including the two-staff keyboard realization of the figured bass that the 1903 edition added. This realization is DdT's, not Buxtehude's. State which view an analysis uses:

- **Solo parts and continuo:** staves 1–3 (violin, viola da gamba, figured bass), or staves 1–4 in App. II (two violins, gamba, figured bass). In App. III, staves 1–3 are gamba, violone and figured bass.
- **DdT's complete texture:** all staves, including the two realization staves (4–5, or 5–6 in App. II).

Per-work staff roles are listed under `staff_roles` in [`validation/editorial-works.json`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/validation/editorial-works.json), keyed by staff number, with the labels `violin`, `violin_1`, `violin_2`, `viola_da_gamba`, `violone`, `figured_continuo_bass`, and `keyboard_realization_upper`/`keyboard_realization_lower` for DdT's realization. A selection of the solo and continuo staves still comes from this corrected DdT text: it is not a transcription of the original partbooks. See the [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#staff-layout-and-analytical-views).

### Sounding pitch

Every note without a printed accidental that sounds sharp or flat carries `@accid.ges` (on its `<accid>` child, if it has one), derived from CAMAT's resolution of the written notation: the key signature, printed accidentals held for their staff, letter and octave to the barline, and ties. A note with a printed accidental, including one that DdT prints in round brackets, takes its pitch from that accidental and carries no `@accid.ges`. Verovio MIDI and music21's MEI import read pitch only from each note's own values, so a reader of sounding pitch should take `@accid.ges` where present and the written accidental otherwise. The [encoding profile](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/mei-encoding-profile.md#sounding-pitch-and-midi) states the rule.

MIDI instruments follow the staff roles, and the files carry no tempo values, so a MIDI export plays at ♩ = 120 unless its reader sets a tempo.

### Finding the facsimile

Every `measure/@facs` points to a measure zone on a `surface`. Each surface stands for one DdT page: its `@corresp` is the BSB IIIF canvas and its `graphic/@target` the IIIF image. Printed DdT page *p* is BSB image *p* + 10, so page 3 is image `00013`. The images stay on the BSB server; this repository does not copy them. The full digitized volume is at [digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00023199](https://digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00023199) (URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00023199-0`).

A web viewer is live at https://egorpol.github.io/DdT_1_vol_11/. It is rebuilt from `main`, not from the tag, so it may show later changes than this release. To see score and facsimile side by side locally, open `mei_facsimile_viewer.ipynb` (requires CAMAT, see below), or load a file into [mei-friend](https://mei-friend.mdw.ac.at/).

### Listening

The viewer's Playback setting is off by default. Turn it on to play the score from the pinned measure or the page on show. The figured bass starts muted, because DdT's realization doubles at least 97 % of its notes. Tempo guide values are suggestions and can be changed in the viewer. The player's samples load from jsDelivr on the first Play, about 5.6 MB for the three instruments of a sonata. A sound outside the work's encoded set asks before it downloads.

### Editorial annotations

Annotations sit inside the measure they concern, as `annot[@type='editorial']`. `@label` gives the category, `@plist` the affected events, `@resp` the responsible editor and `@source` the witness that attests the reading, if any. A sign in square brackets is the edition's, not DdT's; a sign in round brackets is DdT's. The categories are defined in the [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#correction-categories).

The consistency reports and their provenance, [`validation/editorial-reports.json`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/validation/editorial-reports.json), come from a full run completed 2026-11-10 (04:44 UTC) in review mode:

- Software: [CAMAT](https://pypi.org/project/camat/) 0.2.7 from PyPI, Verovio 6.3.0, lxml 6.1.2, saxonche 13.0.0, Python 3.14. Official MEI 5.1 CMN schema, pinned in [`schemas/`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/tree/v0.12.0/schemas) (SHA-256 `f6440d5e…`). Edition profile `DdT_1_vol_11` 0.1.0 ([`validation/profile.json`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/validation/profile.json)).
- All 17 files passed XML and reference checks, RELAX NG, Schematron, resource and image-URL checks, full Verovio rendering and CAMAT event parsing. Their hashes were unchanged after the run.
- The edition's publication rules return **needs-review** for all 17, as expected for unfinalized files. The reports contain 286 rows (276 `warning`, 10 `info`), all explained below under known limitations: 181 Schematron advisories on the IIIF canvas links (one per surface) plus 17 matching edition reminders, 78 short-voice warnings in the passages kept as `metcon="false"`, and 10 info rows on spelling or punctuation variants of tempo and other directions, such as `(Allegro.)` beside `Allegro`.
- The provenance file records each input and report hash, the CAMAT version and source hash, dependency versions and the checks that ran.
- The test suites pass (123 Python and 35 JavaScript tests). `scripts/regenerate_accid_ges.py --check` and `scripts/normalize_midi_data.py --check` find nothing to change. `scripts/musical_lint.py` lists 168 suspects; each is a row to check on the printed page, not an error.

The release-mode gate was not applied: it accepts only finalized files with a recorded, hash-bound reviewer sign-off, which this pre-release does not claim.

To rerun the checks on this release:

```sh
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/validate_corpus.py --manifest validation/editorial-works.json --mode review --verovio --camat-parse
python scripts/regenerate_accid_ges.py --check
python scripts/normalize_midi_data.py --check
python -m pytest -p no:cacheprovider -q tests
node --test tests/js/*.test.mjs
```

The pinned versions need Python 3.12 or newer, and the checks also need libxml2's `xmllint` on `PATH`. The [tool guide](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/validation/README.md) explains each check and report column. An empty or passing report means only that the automated checks found nothing; it does not establish that every pitch, rhythm or figure matches the source.

## Known limitations

- **Not finalized.** No work has a reviewer sign-off, filenames keep `_corr`, and each header's `pubStmt/identifier[@type='URI']` points to the file on `main`, not to this release. Cite the release tag (below) for a fixed version. Whether 1.0 covers 16 or 17 works is still open.
- **Transcription errors can remain, and their number is not measured.** The comparison of continuo and left hand found errors in 48 bars that the earlier correction had missed. It reaches only the notes those two staves share. The violin, gamba and violone parts and the keyboard's right hand, 77,451 of the 106,341 notes (73 %), have no such partner, and errors of the same kind there have not been looked for in this way. No accuracy figure is claimed for any part of the text.
- **Selective witness comparison.** The edition is based on DdT 1903. The Uppsala prints and manuscripts were consulted for selected passages in 16 works, and only where a reading was in question; Op. I/5 lists DdT alone. No complete collation is claimed, and the figures were not systematically compared with the sources.
- **Conjectural emendations.** 54 of the 98 annotations record the editor's judgment, not a reading attested in a source. Each names DdT's reading, so a reader can restore it.
- **Measures marked `metcon="false"`** (28) are intentional: the 12 pickup and complementary ending measures of App. I, and Op. II/2 mm. 199–214, where a 12/8 part sounds against common-time accompaniment. They account for the 78 short-voice warnings in the reports. The MEI headers and annotations explain them.
- **IIIF canvas warnings.** The official Schematron reads `surface/@corresp` as a local ID and flags each external canvas URL, once per surface (181). How to treat them is a decision for 1.0; no waiver is configured.
- **Rendering in Verovio 6.3.0** (the first two are detailed in [`docs/issues.md`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/issues.md)):
  - Op. I/3, m. 17: one printed C4 notehead shared by two voices is drawn as two heads;
  - Op. I/7, m. 250: the parentheses around the 6–4–2 figure stack are drawn beside the top and bottom figures, not around the stack;
  - in a chord, a bracketed sign is usually drawn above the chord, so only the annotation says which note it belongs to;
  - in a bar with a measure rest, a clef change is drawn after the rest, whatever the order in the MEI (Op. I/7 mm. 160 and 201);
  - Op. II/7, mm. 1–2: DdT draws one pair of round brackets across the gamba's A4s; Verovio draws no bracket across several notes, so each A4 has its own pair, and an annotation records DdT's single pair.
- **Playback.** The optional Da capo at the end of Op. II/5 is not played. Every sample stops after 3.13 s, so long notes end early, most audibly on organ and strings, and notes held across the start point sound again from their attack. On a slow computer a page turn may make the sound stutter. The sampled instruments are not tuned to one another: the violin samples average about 3.5 cents flat and the harpsichord samples about 8 cents sharp, and the viewer plays them uncorrected, because a blind comparison showed no audible gain from correcting them ([record](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.12.0/docs/viewer-midi-plan.md)).
- **Images.** The STIMU 1991 scans of the Uppsala prints are not published; only the twenty excerpts in [`supplemental_material/`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/tree/v0.12.0/supplemental_material) are included, under the quotation basis described there. Of the Düben manuscripts, only the five one-bar excerpts are published, under the library's permission.
