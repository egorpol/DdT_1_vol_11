# DdT 1, Vol. 11 — 0.11.0 (pre-release)

This is the third public release of the MEI edition of *Dietrich Buxtehudes Instrumentalwerke* (Denkmäler deutscher Tonkunst, first series, vol. 11, ed. Carl Stiehl, 1903). It has the same 17 works as 0.10.0, each a complete MEI 5.1 file with every measure linked to the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek facsimile. New in this release are 53 editorial decisions on accidental clashes that DdT prints, the corrections of the passages 0.10.0 listed as open, edited MIDI data, and playback in the web viewer. The licences have also changed; see Licences below.

**Status: corrected, not finalized.** This is a review snapshot of 2026-11-05. The musical text and the facsimile links of every work have been reviewed against DdT 1903, and their consistency reports are complete. Publication sign-off, final filenames, the release-mode gate and whether 1.0 covers 16 or 17 works are still open. The [1.0 checklist](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/finalization-plan.md) lists what remains. The files can be used and cited now, but details may change before 1.0. Each change will be listed in the [changelog](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/CHANGELOG.md).

### Sounding pitch

Comparing the values Verovio's MIDI reads for the 106,326 notes that are in both releases: 57 have a corrected pitch name or octave (44 of them in Op. II/3, 38 through two missing alto clefs in mm. 42–44); 52 change from natural to sharp or flat; 18 change from sharp or flat to natural. Ten notes are new, all in Op. II/3. The `@accid.ges` values were regenerated after each change, so they still follow the written notation.

### Accidental clashes that DdT prints

In places DdT prints a note against a differently inflected note of the same letter in another staff, sounding together or overlapping. This release records 53 decisions on such clashes. Each has an editorial annotation that names DdT's reading and the reason, so DdT's text stays recoverable. A sign the edition adds stands in square brackets (`<accid func="edit" enclose="brack"/>`). The [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#recording-decisions-in-mei) states the rule.

- **Clashes at the same moment:** 41 decisions in 14 works, with 44 bracketed signs: 38 conjectural emendations and 3 source-supported by the gamba partbooks of the prints (Op. I/3 m. 169, I/6 m. 13, Op. II/1 m. 24). The musical lint ([`scripts/musical_lint.py`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/scripts/musical_lint.py), with CAMAT) found 46 clashes. An AI model (OpenAI Codex) read the DdT signs in the first triage pass, and Anthropic Claude read Op. II/3 m. 227 in the second. The editor read the Op. I print; Codex read the Op. II print, and Claude checked it. The editor decided all 46 on a review page that showed the DdT crop beside the encoded and the emended bar. The 41 decisions cover 45 clashes; the 46th, Op. I/6 m. 10, was an encoding error.
- **Clashes in the keyboard realization where the two notes start apart:** 7 conjectural emendations, found by a scan for held notes that clash with an entering note: Op. I/3 mm. 6 and 13, Op. II/3 mm. 196 and 197, Op. II/4 m. 59, Op. II/5 m. 7 and Op. II/7 m. 191. Six are bracketed signs. In Op. I/3 m. 6 DdT's E-sharp4 becomes F-sharp4, as the figure asks, and keeps its printed sharp. Codex read the signs from the page images and Claude checked the crops; the editor decided all seven. The readings in Op. II/5 m. 7 and Op. II/7 m. 191 are the editor's own.
- **Clashes in the parts Buxtehude wrote:** 5 source-supported emendations from the same scan, each decided from a historical source: Op. I/4 m. 105, Op. I/7 mm. 201–203, Op. II/3 mm. 167 and 168, and App. III m. 37. In Op. I/7 the Op. I print has a bass clef before m. 201 that DdT lacks, which moves the gamba's notes in mm. 202–203 to D4, D4, A♯3, B3, C♯4. In Op. I/4 the attestation is the print's key signature together with an unsigned note, not a written flat. App. III m. 37 is the first use of a manuscript for this work: the Uppsala parts Instr. mus. i hskr. 13:24 (Düben Collection) have the sharp. Codex read the sources and Claude checked the crops; the editor decided all five.

Three `source-reading` annotations also record where a print differs from DdT and the edition keeps DdT: the figures in Op. I/3 m. 79, and the figures and a bass note in Op. II/4 mm. 125–127.

## Using the edition

### Choosing staves for analysis

- **DdT's complete texture:** all staves, including the two realization staves (4–5, or 5–6 in App. II).

See the [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#staff-layout-and-analytical-views).

### Sounding pitch

Every note without a printed accidental that sounds sharp or flat carries `@accid.ges` (on its `<accid>` child, if it has one), derived from CAMAT's resolution of the written notation: the key signature, printed accidentals held for their staff, letter and octave to the barline, and ties. A note with a printed accidental takes its pitch from that accidental and carries no `@accid.ges`. Verovio MIDI and music21's MEI import read pitch only from each note's own values, so a reader of sounding pitch should take `@accid.ges` where present and the written accidental otherwise. The [encoding profile](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/mei-encoding-profile.md#sounding-pitch-and-midi) states the rule.

MIDI instruments follow the staff roles, and the files carry no tempo values, so a MIDI export plays at ♩ = 120 unless its reader sets a tempo.

### Finding the facsimile

Every `measure/@facs` points to a measure zone on a `surface`. Each surface stands for one DdT page: its `@corresp` is the BSB IIIF canvas and its `graphic/@target` the IIIF image. Printed DdT page *p* is BSB image *p* + 10, so page 3 is image `00013`. The images stay on the BSB server; this repository does not copy them. The full digitized volume is at [digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00023199](https://digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00023199) (URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00023199-0`).

A web viewer is live at https://egorpol.github.io/DdT_1_vol_11/. It is rebuilt from `main`, not from the tag, so it may show later changes than this release. To see score and facsimile side by side locally, open `mei_facsimile_viewer.ipynb` (requires CAMAT, see below), or load a file into [mei-friend](https://mei-friend.mdw.ac.at/).

### Listening

The viewer's Playback setting is off by default. Turn it on to play the score from the pinned measure or the page on show. The figured bass starts muted, because Stiehl's realization doubles at least 97 % of its notes. Tempo guide values are suggestions and can be changed in the viewer. The player's samples load from jsDelivr on the first Play, about 5.6 MB for the three instruments of a sonata. A sound outside the work's encoded set asks before it downloads.

### Editorial annotations

Annotations sit inside the measure they concern, as `annot[@type='editorial']`. `@label` gives the category, `@plist` the affected events, `@resp` the responsible editor and `@source` the witness that attests the reading, if any. A sign in square brackets is the edition's, not DdT's. The categories are defined in the [editorial policy](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/editorial-policy.md#correction-categories).

An empty or passing report means only that the automated checks found nothing; it does not establish that every pitch, rhythm or figure matches the source.

## Known limitations

- **Not finalized.** No work has a reviewer sign-off, filenames keep `_corr`, and each header's `pubStmt/identifier[@type='URI']` points to the file on `main`, not to this release. Cite the release tag (below) for a fixed version. Whether 1.0 covers 16 or 17 works is still open.
- **Selective witness comparison.** The edition is based on DdT 1903. The Uppsala prints and manuscripts were consulted for selected passages in 13 works (Op. I/1–4, I/6, I/7, Op. II/1–4, II/6, App. II and App. III), and only where a reading was in question. Op. I/5, II/5, II/7 and App. I list DdT alone. No complete collation is claimed, and the figures were not systematically compared with the prints.
- **Conjectural emendations.** 54 of the 80 annotations record the editor's judgment, not a reading attested in a source. Each names DdT's reading, so a reader can restore it.
- **App. II, m. 156, violin II** is an interim conjectural emendation (annotation `ayvjvlf1` in the App. II file): one eighth rest is omitted to restore the 3/4 meter. The reading of the Uppsala manuscript has not been confirmed.
- **Measures marked `metcon="false"`** (28) are intentional: the 12 pickup and complementary ending measures of App. I, and Op. II/2 mm. 199–214, where a 12/8 part sounds against common-time accompaniment. They account for the 78 short-voice warnings in the reports. The MEI headers and annotations explain them.
- **IIIF canvas warnings.** The official Schematron reads `surface/@corresp` as a local ID and flags each external canvas URL, once per surface (181). How to treat them is a decision for 1.0; no waiver is configured.
- **Rendering in Verovio 6.3.0** (the first two are detailed in [`docs/issues.md`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/issues.md)):
  - Op. I/3, m. 17: one printed C4 notehead shared by two voices is drawn as two heads;
  - Op. I/7, m. 250: the parentheses around the 6–4–2 figure stack are drawn beside the top and bottom figures, not around the stack;
  - in a chord, a bracketed sign is drawn above the chord, so only the annotation says which note it belongs to;
  - in a bar with a measure rest, a clef change is drawn after the rest, whatever the order in the MEI (Op. I/7 mm. 160 and 201).
- **Playback.** The optional Da capo at the end of Op. II/5 is not played. Every sample stops after 3.13 s, so long notes end early, most audibly on organ and strings, and notes held across the start point sound again from their attack. On a slow computer a page turn may make the sound stutter. The sampled instruments are not tuned to one another: the violin samples average about 3.5 cents flat and the harpsichord samples about 8 cents sharp, and the viewer plays them uncorrected, because a blind comparison showed no audible gain from correcting them ([record](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/blob/v0.11.0/docs/viewer-midi-plan.md)).
- **Images.** The STIMU 1991 scans of the Uppsala prints are not published; only the eleven excerpts in [`supplemental_material/`](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11/tree/v0.11.0/supplemental_material) are included, under the quotation basis described there. No image of the Düben manuscripts is published.
