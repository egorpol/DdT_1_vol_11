# Changelog

All notable changes to this edition are recorded here. Version numbers follow the [encoding profile](docs/mei-encoding-profile.md#release-identity-versions-and-citation): patch releases for compatible corrections or metadata fixes that keep event IDs, minor releases for added works or coverage, and major releases for changes to event identity, segmentation or analytical interpretation. Musical corrections are listed even when the version step is small.

### Changed

- The README and the [editorial policy](docs/editorial-policy.md) now name DdT’s Vorbericht: it belongs to the DdT source, its list points to readings but does not attest them, and no encoded reading rests on it alone.
- Annotations in 16 works now call the keyboard realization “DdT’s”, not “the DdT editor’s”, for the reason given for the viewer label below: 51 sentences read “The keyboard realization is DdT's, not Buxtehude's”, and 2 brackets after “DdT's keyboard realization” drop the phrase. Text only: no notes or IDs change.
- The viewer's Parts menu now lists the realization as “Realization (DdT)”, and its note, the README and [`docs/viewer-midi-plan.md`](docs/viewer-midi-plan.md) say “DdT's realization” in place of “Stiehl's realization”, which was an inference from Stiehl's editorship: the volume's title page, *Vorbericht*, *Inhalt* and the heads of pp. 3, 79, 160, 164 and 176 name no realizer. The 0.11.0 entry below and that release's notes keep their sentence as published, and a dated note at the end of those notes says so. The viewer's header now gives the abbreviation DdT.

## [0.11.0] — 2026-11-05

Third public pre-release. All 17 works are **corrected, not finalized**: a review snapshot of 2026-11-05. Whether 1.0 covers 16 or 17 works is still open. See the [release notes](docs/release-notes/0.11.0.md) for the validation record and known limitations, and the [1.0 checklist](docs/finalization-plan.md) for what remains. 34 `xml:id`s present in v0.10.0 are gone, two of them notes (none of a chord or measure); the list is in the release notes. This is the first release under the licences of 2 November 2026: CC BY 4.0 for the encodings, data and documentation, MIT for the code.

### Added

- Playback in the web viewer (`site/player.js`), behind a Playback setting that is off by default and remembered. Play starts at the pinned measure, else at the first measure of the page on show; the sounding notes are marked in the score and their measure on the facsimile, and both turn with the music. Parts has a switch and a 0–100 % volume per part, remembered by staff role; the figured bass starts muted, because Stiehl's realization doubles at least 97 % of its notes. Tempo scales the whole work from 25 to 200 %. Repeats, on by default and remembered, plays each repeated strain of App. I, Op. II/2 and II/3 twice and is offered only for those works; off sets Verovio's `expandNever` and reloads the work, leaving the score unchanged. Nothing loads until the first Play; then Tone.js 15.1.22, @tonejs/midi 2.0.28 and smplr 1.1.0, and the FluidR3_GM samples of midi-js-soundfonts at a pinned commit, load from jsDelivr with integrity hashes, about 5.6 MB for the three instruments of a sonata. Notes go to the browser's audio 0.3 s ahead so that page turns do not interrupt them, so Play, Tempo and tempo-guide changes take effect about 0.3 s late, and the next page is drawn ahead during playback; on a slow computer a page turn may still make the sound stutter. The Parts menu states this and the other limitations: the optional Da capo at the end of Op. II/5 is not played, every sample stops after 3.13 s, so long notes end early (at 100 % of the tempo guide, 250 string and 864 keyboard notes), most audibly on organ and strings, and notes held across the start point sound again from their attack. The footer credits the libraries and the samples. `scripts/check_site.py` checks the player in headless Chromium, or with `BROWSER=firefox` in Firefox (one clock for sound and marks, page follow, part gains, tempo changes, no late note at a first-visit page turn), and `tests/js/` holds its node tests. Firefox lacks two Web Audio features that Tone.js supplies only to the audio contexts it creates itself, the listener's position and orientation parameters and `cancelAndHoldAtTime`; the player provides stand-ins, without which Play stopped in Firefox with “param must be an AudioParam”. The pinned contrabass file is silent from B♭3 up; the player leaves those samples out, so the 135 notes from B♭3 to E4 in App. III's violone part, which were silent, play the A3 sample raised by up to seven semitones. The MEI is unchanged.
- Sound choice per part and for All parts in the viewer: encoded instrument, Piano, Harpsichord, Church organ or String ensemble, remembered by role without changing the MEI or MIDI. A sample file outside the work's encoded set requires Allow download first, with its size and jsDelivr named; agreement is remembered per file, or for the visit if storage is blocked. Piano (1.7 MB), Church organ (1.7 MB) and String ensemble (1.8 MB) ask; Harpsichord is already used by every work. Playback continues during loading, old notes finish on their previous sound, mute and volume carry over, and failed loads keep the previous sound. The browser keeps sample files in its cache; decoded sounds occupy memory and live loading may stutter on a slow computer.
- A tempo guide for the player in `playback/tempo-guide/`, one CSV per work: a suggested speed for each of the 109 tempo sections of the 17 works, as a percentage of ♩ = 120, from a rule table of tempo words, meters and note values given in its README. The editor checked the 38 flagged rows by ear on 2026-11-02 and kept every value. They are playback suggestions, not the edition's reading, and stay out of the MEI. `scripts/build_site.py` checks every row against the MEI and embeds the guide in the viewer, whose Tempo guide menu lets readers change each section and copy their changes.
